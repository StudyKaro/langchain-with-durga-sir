*** 7 Engineering Questions ***
===============================
**1. Engineering:**
-------------------
1. Are you able to provide 10x more?
2. Don't ask, "How can I earn more?" First ask, "How can I become more valuable?"
3. Your goal should not be to impress people with what you know. Your goal should be to improve their lives with what you know.
4. Never compete only on price when you can compete on value.
5. Ordinary efforts may help you survive. Extraordinary value can help you stand out.
6. "Friends, if the world can easily replace your contribution, your biggest responsibility is to improve your contribution."
7. What is one skill or service in which I can become significantly better over the next 12 months?
8. *Can you create breakthrough technology instead of incremental improvements?*
A viable new business *shouldn't just be 10% better than the competition; it needs to be 10x better*.
8. Example : Real state  
-  Plot, Registry, Law, Contractor, Interior, Rent(tenant), Re-sale, Support

**2. Timing:** 
---------------
1. *Is now the right time to start your particular business?*
3. A wonderful opportunity has little value if you never take action.
2. Being too early without preparation is risky. Being too late without a difference is also risky.
3. Don't wait for the perfect moment. Find the right moment to take the next sensible step.
4. The best time to develop a future skill is before you urgently need it.
5. Yesterday's success cannot guarantee tomorrow's opportunities.
6. Preparation gives you the ability to recognise and use opportunities.
7. You cannot control when an opportunity comes. But you can control how prepared you are.
8. Sometimes one small decision taken today can change the direction of your next ten years.

```Friends, five years from today, you may regret not starting the journey you could have started with just five minutes today.```

**3.The Monopoly Question:**
----------------------------
1. Are you starting with a big share of a small market?
2. The goal is to avoid massive, hyper-competitive markets on day one.
3. Instead, you want to identify a highly specific, niche target audience, completely dominate that small sector, and then expand outward.
2. Are you becoming No.1 in a small area first...
3. or trying to fight everyone in a big market?


**4. The People Question:**
---------------------------
1. Do you have the right team to build this business?
2. Are the people working with you capable of making this business successful?
3. Good Idea + Wrong Team = Failure
4. Good Idea + Right Team = Success
5. Excellent story + worst actor = failure
6. Average story + Best actor = success

**5. The Distribution Question**
--------------------------------
1. *Do you have a way to not just create but deliver your product?*
2. Great engineering does not sell itself. You must have a reproducible, scalable mechanism for getting your product into the hands of users, whether that is through enterprise sales channels, viral product-led growth, or strategic partnerships.

**6. The Durability Question**
------------------------------
1. *Will your market position be defensible 10 and 20 years into the future?*
2. You need a "moat" to protect your business from future competitors. This could be network effects, proprietary algorithms, deep integrations into client systems that make switching difficult, or a strong brand reputation.

**7. The Secret Question**
--------------------------
1. *Have you identified a unique opportunity that others don't see?*
2. Great businesses are built on truths that are not yet widely accepted. This is the unique insight or hidden market dynamic that your competitors have overlooked, giving you a fundamental advantage from day one.
